# Jason Murray (@jason_swet): channel, frameworks and Iris™

Research for the Visual Taxonomy project, 2026-11-23.
Sources: his TikTok profile (304 posts; captions and stats for 299 of them, auto-transcripts for most; 5 stayed blocked), his 23-minute YouTube video "Art Direction Techniques You Can Use", jasonmurray.com/links, and the public Iris page at visuals.modernartdirection.com.
Not seen: the Iris member canvas (invite-only), his newsletter archive, video frames (only transcripts and captions were read).

## TL;DR

1. **His core move: classify the mechanism, not the aesthetic.** He doesn't tag images "Y2K" or "surreal". He tags *how* they communicate (pattern break, form substitution, gaze cue), "regardless of the subject, medium or aesthetic". That makes references reusable across brands that look nothing alike.
2. **Iris™ is that idea as a product.** It's a three-level map: **Operation → Domain → Technique**, with 5 operations, 6 domains and 46 techniques in the public page code. It links images through shared techniques. It's in invite-only beta with his bootcamp students. It's **not an image generator**, and pricing and launch date aren't announced.
3. **"Taste" in his framing is a skill ladder, not a verdict.** First consume widely. Then say what you like, name the feeling, find a comparison ("feels like a Michel Gondry movie"), and finally name the specific ingredients ("flash lighting", "hand-drawn mixed media"). Taste is also a spectrum (mainstream, cultural, counter, elite, commercial), so "good" depends on the audience.
4. **His content wins on saves, not views.** Taste, technique and tool-list posts have median save rates around 4%, and the best ones reach 7–10%. His sponsored Adobe reels reached 2.9M–7.3M plays but were saved by under 0.1% of viewers. 2026, the taste and technique year, is his best year by median plays and saves. A "too X? try Y" format turns the taxonomy into a lookup table people keep.
5. **The gap for you:** Iris stops at understanding. Your system can go one step further, from technique to generation prompt to on-brand output, scored by *your* approvals per brand. That bridge is the part he says he isn't building.

## 3. The reel formats he repeats

1. **The removal test.** "Why is this image so much more interesting than this one?" He shows the ad, removes the technique, and the idea vanishes. Then he names the technique and closes with "We're inundated with visuals that don't actually say much… follow along if you want to make yours mean something." *Used for: semantic compounding (Land Rover), pattern break (VW).*
2. **Symptom → fix.** "Too ordinary? Try frame within a frame. Too scattered? Gaze cue. Too busy? Isolation in void. Too obvious? Figure-ground reversal. Visuals don't mean anything? Genre quotation. Too literal? Form substitution." Short (17–25 s), heavily saved.
3. **Three techniques.** Name, a one-line definition and one example each. 25–70 s.
4. **Artist dissection.** One maker, 2 techniques: Szilveszter Makó = visual quotation (painting) + visible *facture* (paint strokes show).
5. **Lineage trace.** Follow a look back through time: macro textures that read as landscapes (Decathlon ← Toyota ← a 2015 tennis ad ← Edward Weston, 1930s ← Percy Smith). Hockney joiners and panography. His advice is to branch off from further back in the family tree.
6. **Essay series.** *The Theory of Taste*, one extended metaphor (food) pushed across 5 episodes.
7. **Brand teardown.** Gap, Zeekr × Taika Waititi, Nike ACG, Polaroid, Squarespace, Coinbase.

What performs: see section 8 and `research/channel-stats.md`.

## 4. Iris™: the taxonomy of visuals

**Pitch:** "Understand the idea behind the image." Explore the visual techniques behind a message, connect references, and find a new direction.

**Structure** (from the public landing page and its demo; the member canvas wasn't seen):

```
Operation (what the image does to your mind)
  └─ Domain (the channel it works through)
       └─ Technique (the specific mechanism)
            └─ Images (examples, with an explanation of what the technique does in each)
```

- **5 operations** (public code keys): `differentiate`, `group`, `sequence`, `associate`, `predict`. The demo shows `predict` labelled **"Infer"**.
- **6 domains:** `feature`, `gestalt`, `space`, `social`, `semantic`, `cultural`.
- **46 techniques** (keys in the page's icon set): symbol cue, brand coding, persona, in-scene text, displacement, genre quotation, chiaroscuro, rim light, complementary, simultaneous (contrast), silhouette, depth of field, isolation, scale disparity, warm/cool, texture, juxtaposition, pop-out, proximity, similarity, common region, continuity, symmetry, figure-ground, pareidolia, leading lines, gaze, vector, hierarchy, reading path, central bias, frame within, pattern break, closure, crop, occlusion, forced perspective, genre, substitution, caricature, saturation block, facture, compounding, contextual (metaphor), personification, temporal synthesis.
- **Confirmed mappings** (the demo's 5 examples):
  - Infer → Semantic → Form substitution (seashells replace earbuds)
  - Infer → Semantic → Contextual metaphor (pearls eaten like food)
  - Infer → Semantic → Semantic compounding (orchid and cables)
  - Differentiate → Feature → Pop-out (one runner in orange)
  - Differentiate → Feature → Silhouette (figure behind red translucent surface)
- In his launch reel: "say you want something to stand out: that's an operation we can call *differentiate*. You could differentiate through visible features like colour or shape, or through meaning: *semantic contrast*." The model is biological classification (domain, kingdom, phylum…).
- **Positioning (from the FAQ):**
  - Not a mood board (that gathers a look) and not a mind map (that connects anything). Iris connects images *through techniques*, and focuses on visual communication.
  - Mostly ads, posters and magazine covers, because those images "have a clear job".
  - No single correct reading. It's a working taxonomy, and you can question or edit its readings.
  - Not a generator. Analysis tools "suggest techniques and connections in images you add".
  - Rights: membership gives tools, not a licence to the artworks.
- **Status:** invited members and eligible students only. Waitlist open; pricing and date not announced. He has said it may never go fully public.

**Where your system differs:** Iris is a *learning* tool for art directors. Yours is a *production* tool for three brands. Same idea layer, plus a look layer, a brand-taste layer and a path to generation.

## 5. His frameworks (the parts worth stealing)

### 5.1 Big idea pyramid
**Insight** (an unspoken human truth) → **Idea** (the verb: what we do with that truth; fits on a post-it, like "You're not you when you're hungry") → **Executions** (the most detail, every format). Every execution ladders back to the insight. His *idea sizing* chart ranks scale: tactic < one-off < execution < campaign < platform.

### 5.2 Ingredients and the feeling ↔ solution spectrum
- An art director's "tool" is **ingredients**: colour, lighting, texture, depth of field, casting, medium (photo/illustration/CGI), lettering, set, music.
- **Visual feeling** side: copy carries the message and the ingredients make you *feel* it. Polaroid's handwritten ballpoint pen, Diesel's flash party photography, Oatly's blocky type.
- **Visual solution** side: the pairing of ingredients *is* the message, with little or no copy. Lego bricks whose shadows are a boat or a dinosaur; a knife cutting through the board; Twitter's hashtag + any cultural subject.
- **2–3 standout ingredients.** A dish has many ingredients but two or three flavours you notice. Restraint.

### 5.3 Visual devices
A repeatable formula you can slot endless subjects into: [brand mark + subject (+ optional copy)]. Time's red border around a small piece of a historic photo; Google Year in Search. Risk: if the logic isn't instantly clear, people stay confused.

### 5.4 Visual tension
Pick 2–3 ingredients and stretch them apart like a rubber band. Too far and it snaps; too many directions and it's just a bigger circle.

### 5.5 Visual lineage
Every visual element carries associations. You can borrow them, or they're baggage to steer around. Trace an inspiring reference back, and branch off from an older ancestor to land somewhere more original.

### 5.6 The Theory of Taste (5 episodes)
1. **Eat widely.** Replace scrolling with digging and escape with exploration; go outside your lane.
2. **Articulate.** Like/dislike → feeling ("uncomfy") → simile/metaphor ("like a Michel Gondry movie") → specific ingredients ("flash lighting", "hand-drawn mixed media"). Name a collection after each ingredient: "filling my pantry".
3. **Fluency through immersion.** Go to the source: artist reps and agencies. A mood board must be *executable*: know who can make it.
4. **Spectrum of taste.** Mainstream, cultural (you can serve it but not claim it), counter, elite and commercial ("a little flavour of aspiration mixed with something familiar and inviting").
5. **Taste → vision.** Taste judges what exists; vision imagines what doesn't. "Don't just ask what do I like. Ask what would you like to see?"

### 5.7 Sourcing references
Film-still databases (FilmGrab, ShotDeck, FRAMESET, Film Shot Cafe), Are.na (sorted by time, not algorithm, with sources shown), artist-rep sites (e.g. Art + Commerce archive), Cosmos over Pinterest (easier to find the source), Dave Dye's Pinterest boards. Always keep the source link.

## 6. Technique glossary (as he explains them)

| Technique | His definition (paraphrased) | His example |
|---|---|---|
| Semantic compounding | Different subjects or symbols combined to say a bigger idea | Passport stamps forming a Land Rover Defender |
| Pattern break | Establish repetition, then interrupt it; the change carries the idea | VW: rows of identical poses, one man slouching |
| Occlusion | One thing hides part of another; what you hide changes what it's about | Flowers covering a face (Story mfg. × Crocs) |
| Body vector | A pose or gesture creates direction | Many hands reaching for one cigarette |
| Form substitution | Swap one thing for another of similar shape or texture | KFC chicken as the rocket fire under a shuttle |
| Figure-ground reversal | Positive and negative space both read as subject | "Turn the subtraction into the subject" |
| Gaze cue | Where someone looks tells us where to look | Burger King (Nixon Freire) |
| Frame within a frame | An opening or boundary inside the image frames the subject | Elliott Erwitt |
| Isolation in void | Remove distractions, surround with empty space | Carl Fischer / Esquire |
| Scale disparity | Dramatically different sizes together | (3-technique reel) |
| Genre / visual quotation | Borrow the codes of a known genre, era, format or work | Makó borrowing painting; the 4×4 grid trend |
| Visible facture | The process of making is visible | Makó's paint strokes; Polaroid's ballpoint pen |
| Contextual metaphor | An object takes another's role in the scene | Zeekr: the hanging car *is* the spotlight |
| Leading lines | Lines guide the eye | Zeekr headlight beams |
| Visual literalism | Render the phrase word for word | Frozen margaritas = a fridge full of daisies |
| Brand mark + subject | A brand asset frames an endless set of subjects | Time magazine's red border |
| Panography / Hockney joiner | Collapse time into one frame from many snapshots | Hockney; sports ads; Converse |

## 7. What this means for your system

1. **Tag the mechanism separately from the look.** Style presets and look recipes (lighting, palette, medium) are *ingredient* recipes. What's missing is the *idea* layer: which technique carries the message. Keep both, as separate fields.
2. **Don't let the model decide "good taste".** By his own framing taste is a spectrum, not a verdict. Let the model *describe* (techniques, ingredients, register). "Good" comes from your approve/reject per brand, so each brand builds its own taste profile. That's his "save what you like, reject what you don't, then name the ingredient" loop, automated.
3. **Use the removal test as the universal quality check.** "Name the technique, remove it: does the message disappear?" If not, the image has no idea yet. This works for scoring generations too.
4. **Turn diagnostics into generation moves.** His "too X? try Y" table is a ready-made prompt-revision tool: a generation comes back "too busy", so re-prompt with *isolation in void*. It's in `taxonomy/seed-taxonomy.yaml` under `diagnostics`.
5. **Bridge technique to prompt.** Each technique in the seed taxonomy has a `prompt_pattern`. This is the part Iris doesn't do ("not an image generator") and the part that saves you the most time.
6. **Keep sources and credit.** Store the source link and credit with every reference. Treat the library as study material, not assets to republish (Iris makes the same rights point).

### Starting ideas per brand (untested; for discussion)

- **VG Saveur butter** (forest green, butter yellow, cream; VG Butler):
  - *Contextual metaphor*: a butter block on jeweller's velvet, in a ring box (butter as a luxury object).
  - *Pattern break*: rows of identical pale blocks, one golden VG block.
  - *Scale disparity*: VG Butler carrying a block twice his size.
  - *Isolation in void + chiaroscuro*: one block under hard flash on a dark ground, so the look has a named job.
- **Gelato:**
  - *Form substitution*: scoops standing in for hot-air balloons or a setting sun.
  - *Occlusion*: a cone hiding a face, so the product becomes the face.
  - *Gaze cue*: everyone in frame looking at the counter.
  - *Visual literalism*: on a claim like "melts in your mouth".
- **Personal brand:**
  - *Genre quotation*: borrow newspaper or documentary codes (halftone, column grids, captions).
  - *Visible facture*: show the process.
  - *A visual device*: one brand mark paired with a changing subject, as a recurring series format, the way he runs *The Theory of Taste*.

## 9. Liquid model fit (for the tagging step)

- **Model:** Liquid **LFM3-VL-2B** (about 1.9B parameters; Liquid reports it beats LFM2.5-VL-3B on their 62-benchmark average, which hasn't been independently checked; first LFM vision model to accept video). GGUF for llama.cpp, needs build b11960 or newer.
- **Small enough for a laptop:** a Q4 model plus an F16 vision projector take a few GB of memory. Speed per image is unmeasured.
- **Expect:** good at the *visible* layer (lighting, palette, medium, depth of field, faces and gaze, text present, composition). Weaker at the *inferential* layer (compounding, contextual metaphor, lineage), which needs world knowledge and reasoning.
- **So:** ask yes/no per technique with "what in the image shows it", not "name the technique". Send low-confidence calls to a review queue. Hand-label about 100 images as a gold set and measure before trusting it.
- **For "find similar":** add an image-embedding model (CLIP/SigLIP family). LFM3-VL describes images but doesn't produce embeddings.
